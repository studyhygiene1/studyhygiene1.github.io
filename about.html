A Systematic Approach to Learning and Test Preparation

I was a C-minus student in math for most of my life. It was the one subject that never clicked, no matter how hard I tried. Then, over one month, I studied math three hours a day, six days a week. On a grading scale where 75% was an A, I scored 100% on the final. That experience is the reason I tutor the way I do: being "bad at math" is almost never true. It usually just means the material hasn't been explained in a way that connects yet — and finding that connection quickly is my job, not the student's.

I bring an unusual mix of training to that work. I hold an M.Sc. in Electrical Engineering, a B.Sc. in Computer Engineering, and a J.D. with active legal licensure. I started in the CBSE system in India, where I was my city's top regional scorer on board exams, then studied in the UK system at Loughborough University before earning a scholarship to the University of Arizona for engineering. Having learned and taught across the Indian, British, and American systems, I've seen the same students get stuck for the same reasons in every one of them — and I've learned to spot it fast.

That range shows up in what I teach: math and physics from arithmetic through calculus, and test prep across the SAT, ACT, ASVAB, GRE, and GED. I also work regularly with law students on IRAC structure, legal writing, and exam prep, drawing on my own courtroom advocacy and mock trial background. On the writing side, I've won public speaking competitions from elementary school through law school — school, state, regional, and national level — and I bring that same focus on clarity, structure, and persuasion to every essay I help a student write.

None of that is what students and parents mention first, though. What they tell me is that I'm patient, I adapt to how they actually learn, and I care whether they succeed. Most of the students who come to me are frustrated or discouraged after struggling elsewhere. My job is to rebuild the understanding — and the confidence — at the same time.
